Part 1 · the format a4's leaders expect

Four pages, same order, every week 11 min live

A weekly pack works because it is boring in the right way: the same four pages in the same order every single week. Readers stop hunting for numbers and start noticing changes - which is the whole point. Page 1 is the scorecard (core four, WoW and YoY). Page 2 is the funnel gates (four gates, WoW). Page 3 is channels (GMV by channel, share, WoW). Page 4 is the narrative - one paragraph plus an actions table. Nothing else.

LiveWhy the order is fixed4 min▶

The order is a funnel of questions. Page 1 answers "how did we do" (the outcome). Page 2 answers "where in the journey did it happen" (the gates). Page 3 answers "which traffic did it come from" (the channels). Page 4 answers "so what" (the story and the actions). A reader who trusts pages 1-3 can skim straight to page 4; a reader who doubts page 4 can trace every claim back through 3, 2, 1. That two-way traceability is what makes a pack more than a screenshot dump.

Real world

Amazon's weekly business review runs on the same discipline: a fixed deck of metrics reviewed in the same order every week, so anomalies pop out by familiarity rather than by highlighting. You are building the small-store version - four pages instead of seventy, but the same principle: format stability is what makes change visible.

Self-studyWho reads which page3 min read▶

Each page has a natural first reader, and knowing who that is tells you how to write it:

  • Page 1 (scorecard) - the CEO and finance. They read the WoW and YoY columns first and the levels second. Round consistently; never change decimal places between weeks.
  • Page 2 (gates) - product and engineering. A gate that moved is their to-do list; a gate that held is their alibi.
  • Page 3 (channels) - marketing. Share tells them the mix, WoW tells them this week's fires.
  • Page 4 (narrative) - everyone, first. In practice the room reads page 4, then flips back to whichever page backs the claim they doubt. Write page 4 knowing it will be audited.
Demo 1 · everyone builds

Assemble this week's pack ★ 15 min · everyone works

The setup: Mango Lane just closed a soft week. To keep every number checkable, we use the canonical baseline you memorized in b1 as "last week" and these actuals as "this week": sessions 356,400 (-1.0%), session CR 2.673% (-1.0%), AOV flat at $68, so GMV lands at $647,741 (-2.0%). Build pages 1-3 now; page 4 (the narrative) is Demo 2.

Orders = sessions x CR: 356,400 x 2.673% = 9,527 (9,526.6 - the pennies are rounding). Orders fell the same -2.0% as GMV because AOV held still.

GMV = orders x AOV: 9,526.6 x $68 = $647,741. Matches the headline. Page 1 agrees with itself.

Cross-foot the WoW: 0.99 (sessions) x 0.99 (CR) x 1.00 (AOV) = 0.9801, i.e. -2.0% GMV. Two small -1% dips multiply into the -2% headline. If your factor changes do not multiply back to the GMV change, a number on the page is wrong - find it before the room does.

YoY cross-foots too: 1.070 x 1.015 x 1.012 = 1.099, matching the +9.9% GMV YoY. Soft week, healthy year.

Self-studyThe pre-ship checklist - three reconciliations3 min read▶

Before any pack leaves your hands, run the same three checks in the same order. They take two minutes and they catch almost everything:

Cross-foot the scorecard. Sessions change x CR change x AOV change must reproduce the GMV change (0.99 x 0.99 x 1.00 = -2.0% this week). Rounding pennies are fine; a tenth of a percent is not.

Multiply the gates. The four gate rates on page 2 must multiply to page 1's CR (0.40 x 0.2475 x 0.45 x 0.60 = 2.673%). If they do not, one gate is stale or one denominator drifted (b8 territory).

Sum the channels. Page 3's rows must total page 1's GMV to the dollar. A gap means untagged traffic is hiding somewhere - find it or footnote it, never silently absorb it.

A pack that fails a reconciliation and ships anyway teaches the room to double-check you forever. A pack that reconciles for ten straight weeks earns the most valuable thing an analyst owns: numbers nobody re-derives.

Demo 2 · everyone writes

Page 4 - the narrative ★ 8 min · everyone works

The narrative is one paragraph in a fixed template: "What moved: [metric, size]. Why: [lever + slice + hypothesis]. What we are doing: [action + owner]. When we will know: [re-check date]." No adjectives, no throat-clearing, every claim traceable to pages 1-3.

LiveThe filled narrative for this week5 min▶
Real world

What moved: GMV fell 2.0% WoW to $647,741 (-$13.2k). Why: the drop is concentrated in the social channel, down 18% (-$11.9k) while other channels held roughly flat; the add-to-cart gate dipped 1% with it. Working hypothesis: a platform algorithm change cut our social reach - impressions on our top posts fell midweek. What we are doing: social lead is rebalancing creative toward formats the feed currently favors and shifting this week's boost budget to the two best-performing posts. When we will know: re-check social GMV and add-to-cart in next Friday's pack.

The whole trick The narrative answers the four questions before the room asks them. A leader reading page 4 should have nothing left to ask except "is the hypothesis right" - and the check date tells them when they will find out. If your paragraph invites the question "but why?", it is not done.
Self-studyNarrative anti-patterns - four ways page 4 goes wrong3 min read▶
  • The weather report. "GMV was down slightly this week amid softer traffic." Describes, explains nothing, commits to nothing. If a sentence could have been written without opening pages 2-3, delete it.
  • The victory lap with no denominator. "Social engagement was strong!" Strong against what? The narrative only speaks in the pack's specced metrics - engagement is not one of them.
  • The hypothesis stated as fact. Write "working hypothesis: platform algorithm change", not "the platform changed its algorithm". The re-check date is what upgrades a hypothesis to a finding.
  • The orphan action. An action with no owner or no check date is a wish. The actions table has three columns for a reason; a row missing any of them does not ship.
Part 4

Weekly vs monthly - what the bigger pack adds 5 min live

The weekly pack manages the run-rate; the monthly pack manages the direction. A monthly pack keeps the same four pages and adds four things: benchmarks (a5's tables - where does our CR sit against IRP and Littledata this month), the returning-customer trend (Shopify's returning customer rate over the last 6 months), the guardrail metrics (margin and return rate - the numbers that stop you celebrating GMV bought with discounts), and one deep dive (a single question worked properly, b5-style). And where the weekly compares WoW first, the monthly compares YoY primarily - month-over-month is too seasonal to trust (a5).

Self-studyPack hygiene - three rules that keep it alive3 min read▶
  • Freeze the format for a quarter. The pack's power is familiarity; a page that moves around resets everyone's pattern-matching to zero. Change format at quarter boundaries, announced.
  • Footnote any definition change. If a metric's definition changes mid-quarter (a6 governance), the pack carries a dated footnote on every affected page until the comparison periods are clean.
  • Never add a page without removing one. Packs die by accretion - every stakeholder wants their pet chart added and nobody ever asks to remove one. Four pages weekly is the budget. A new page must evict an old one.
Homework

Try it yourself - this week ◐ 20-30 min total

  • Build page 1 (the scorecard) for your own store, this week: GMV, sessions, session CR, AOV - each with WoW and YoY.
  • Cross-foot it: multiply your sessions, CR, and AOV changes and confirm they reproduce your GMV change. If they do not, find the leak before you show anyone.
  • Bonus: write the four-sentence narrative for your week using the template, even if the answer is "nothing moved, no action". That sentence is also information.

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · What is the weekly pack's page order, and why is it frozen?

Outcome, then where in the journey, then which traffic, then the story. Frozen format is the feature: familiarity is what makes an anomaly pop. A reshuffled pack resets everyone to hunting.

2 · Sessions fell 1%, CR fell 1%, AOV was flat. What GMV change should the scorecard show, and why?

GMV = sessions x CR x AOV, so changes multiply: 0.99 x 0.99 = 0.9801, about -2.0%. That is the cross-foot: if the scorecard's GMV change does not equal the product of its factor changes, a number is wrong.

3 · What must the page 4 narrative contain?

Four sentences, fixed template. It answers the room's four questions before they are asked; the pages behind it carry the numbers, the paragraph carries the meaning and the commitment to re-check.
